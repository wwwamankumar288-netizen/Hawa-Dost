HEALTH EFFECTS OF AIR POLLUTION ON SOUTH ASIAN POPULATIONS

=== PM2.5 HEALTH EFFECTS ===

ACUTE EFFECTS (within hours of exposure):
- Respiratory: Coughing, wheezing, shortness of breath
- Eye, nose, throat irritation
- Reduced lung function (measurable within 1 hour of exposure at AQI > 200)
- Cardiovascular: Increased heart rate, blood pressure elevation
- Neurological: Headache, fatigue, difficulty concentrating

CHRONIC EFFECTS (months to years of exposure):
- Chronic Obstructive Pulmonary Disease (COPD) — 60% higher risk in Delhi vs WHO-compliant cities
- Lung cancer — PM2.5 classified as Group 1 carcinogen by IARC (2013)
- Cardiovascular disease — atherosclerosis, heart attack, stroke
- Type 2 diabetes — chronic inflammation pathway
- Neurodegenerative disease — Alzheimer's, Parkinson's linked to ultrafine particles

CHILDREN SPECIFIC EFFECTS:
- Stunted lung development — lungs that never reach full capacity
- Lower IQ scores (3-5 points) with high prenatal exposure
- Higher rates of childhood asthma (India COPD Coalition data)
- Increased school absenteeism
- Breathing rate 50% faster than adults = 50% more PM2.5 intake per kg body weight

ELDERLY SPECIFIC EFFECTS:
- PM2.5 enters bloodstream faster due to weakened lung lining
- 10 µg/m³ increase in PM2.5 linked to 1% increase in daily cardiovascular mortality
- Hip fracture risk increases — systemic inflammation affects bone density
- Cognitive decline accelerated by 2-3 years with chronic high exposure

PREGNANCY EFFECTS:
- Low birth weight (most significant risk — 15% increase at high PM2.5)
- Preterm birth (PM2.5 > 60 µg/m³ increases risk by 11%)
- Birth defects (cardiovascular) with first-trimester exposure
- Gestational hypertension

=== PROTECTING YOURSELF ===

MASKS:
N95 respirator effectiveness breakdown:
- When properly fitted: 95% filtration of 0.3 µm particles
- Fit test critical: Pinch nose bridge, check for air leaks at edges
- Breathing resistance increases by 20-30% — take breaks indoors
- Replace every 8 hours of use, or when damp
- N99/FFP3: 99% filtration — better for children and elderly

Proper fitting steps:
1. Cup mask in both hands, nose piece at fingertips
2. Place over nose and chin
3. Mold nose piece to shape of nose bridge
4. Cup both hands over mask and exhale sharply — no air should escape sides
5. Inhale sharply — mask should pull inward slightly

INDOOR AIR QUALITY SAFE LEVELS:
- PM2.5 < 12 µg/m³ — WHO good
- PM2.5 12-35 µg/m³ — moderate, sensitive groups should limit activity
- PM2.5 > 35 µg/m³ — run air purifier, limit cooking and candles
- PM2.5 > 75 µg/m³ — close all windows regardless of heat

MEDICAL GUIDANCE:
- People with asthma: Keep rescue inhaler accessible during high AQI days
- Pre-existing heart disease: Avoid outdoor exertion when AQI > 100
- When to seek emergency care: Severe chest pain, difficulty breathing, bluish lips — call ambulance immediately
- Annual lung function test (spirometry) recommended for people in high-AQI cities

=== FOOD AND NUTRITION TO COMBAT POLLUTION EFFECTS ===

ANTI-INFLAMMATORY FOODS:
- Turmeric (haldi) — curcumin reduces PM2.5 lung inflammation
- Ginger (adrak) — anti-inflammatory, helps clear airways
- Amla (Indian gooseberry) — highest Vitamin C content, antioxidant
- Jaggery (gud) — traditional Indian remedy for lung cleansing
- Garlic — allicin reduces oxidative stress from PM2.5
- Mustard greens (sarson) — Vitamin K + antioxidants

DRINKS:
- Warm turmeric milk (haldi doodh) — classic respiratory remedy
- Tulsi tea — anti-inflammatory + antimicrobial
- Warm water with honey and ginger — soothes irritated airways
- Avoid cold drinks on high-AQI days (constrict airways)

AVOID ON HIGH-AQI DAYS:
- Alcohol — dehydrates lungs, impairs cilia function
- Smoking — combines with PM2.5 for exponential damage
- Heavy fatty meals — increase cardiovascular strain
